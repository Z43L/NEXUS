How it works

From the whole model to one slice per operator

Four steps, from publishing to payment. The design comes from our own simulator; this is not yet a network under real load.

  1. Split and publish.The author cuts the model into S slots (layer ranges), encrypts the chunks, applies k-of-n erasure coding (e.g. 10 of 16, 1.6x overhead) and signs a manifest with a Merkle root. Storage nodes only ever see ciphertext.
  2. One operator, one slot.When registration closes, a random beacon assigns slots in capacity blocks. Each operator is pinned to one, cannot choose which, and none holds more than 1/S of the model. The key is released only to the assigned node.
  3. The request crosses the slots.The OpenAI-compatible gateway takes your call; the coordinator routes it through all S slots and, inside each, splits work in proportion to each node's speed.
  4. Receipt and pay for work.Each node signs a receipt for the work done. The contribution ledger computes each share and the author earns a royalty in settlement batches. v0.1 is a testnet: no real money moves.
Who it is for

Three ways to take part

Developers

Change the URL, keep your code

If you use the OpenAI SDK, point base_url at the NEXUS gateway.

  • Open models, with a signed receipt per job.
  • Performance: slot splitting adds network hops. Simulator estimate: roughly 1.6 to 7 tok/s per stream depending on slot count (see below); think batch jobs rather than interactive chat.
  • Address and key shown are placeholders until the beta opens.

Node operators

Contribute your GPU to a slot

You are paid for verified work, not for keeping the machine on.

  • You are pinned to a single slot, however many nodes you run.
  • Joining a ~2.2 GB slot takes about 60 s at the median (simulator estimate).
  • Your node sees its slice of the model in the clear in memory; unavoidable without a TEE.
  • No income or yield promises. Minimum hardware requirements: [TO BE VERIFIED].
Model authors

Publish and earn royalties

A CLI splits, encrypts, erasure-codes and signs your model's manifest.

  • You set your royalty in the manifest; it is paid out in the settlement batch (testnet).
  • No operator receives the full weights, but this does not protect against coalitions or distillation.
  • You must be allowed to redistribute the model: base-model licenses (Gemma, Llama, etc.) are respected.
Security

Security model, no smoke

This is what NEXUS protects and what it does not. If your case needs the second list, NEXUS v0.1 is not for you yet.

What it protects

  • No operator holds more than 1/S of the model: each is pinned to a single slot.
  • The attacker cannot choose a slot: stratified random assignment after registration closes (0.96 efficiency in the simulator).
  • Weights at rest are encrypted and erasure-coded; storage nodes only see ciphertext.
  • Signed receipts per job and pay proportional to verified work.
  • A regional outage or a large operator leaving degrades capacity instead of taking the service down (simulator).

What it does NOT protect

  • Coalitions: with ~300 nodes per slot, 2% of operators reconstruct the model with P≈0.44 and 5% with P≈0.99 (simulator).
  • Distillation: anyone who can query the model can train a copy from its answers.
  • The compute node sees its slot in the clear in memory; without TEE or MPC this is unavoidable.
  • Your prompts: nodes process your request's activations. Do not send sensitive data.
  • Sybil: stake alone does not protect; without per-node fingerprinting or a fee, collecting a 16-slot model would cost ~$770 (illustrative parameters).
  • Stronger-secrecy options (TEE, per-layer splitting, per-node fingerprinting) are under study, not in v0.1.

With T=1, which is what v0.1 will have, a small coalition is enough. Slicing each layer across mutually distrustful nodes reduces that risk but needs nodes in nearly the same data center (RTT ≈ 0.5 ms; at 2 ms the cost rises to ≈ 1–2 s per token) and is not part of v0.1. Without slicing, 16 slots run at ≈ 2.8 tok/s per stream. More slots speed up recovery but each slot adds a network hop. Coalition resistance does not come from the split alone: it needs an identity cost, TEEs or per-layer slicing.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is NEXUS decentralized?

With caveats. The model is split across independent operators and none holds the whole model, but in the alpha the coordinator, gateway and registry are run by the NEXUS team. We do not say "trustless": you trust the coordinator and that no large coalition of operators forms.

Can the model be copied?

A large enough coalition of operators could reconstruct it, and anyone who can query the API can try to distill it from the answers. NEXUS lowers and raises the cost of that risk (1/S per operator, unpredictable slots); it does not remove it.

Are my prompts private?

Do not assume so. The node running a slice sees that slice of the model in the clear and processes the activations of your request. Do not send sensitive or personal data in the alpha. Stronger secrecy (TEE, per-layer splitting) is under research and not part of v0.1.

Is there a token or cryptocurrency?

No. v0.1 has no token and settlement is tested on a testnet with no real value. If a token were ever studied, its regulatory risks (for example, being treated as a security) would have to be analysed first.

When will it launch and how much will it cost?

No dates or prices are published. It is a closed alpha; pricing will be defined in the beta. Join the waitlist and we will tell you the next step.

Which models will be available?

Open models. Every model in the catalog ships with its license and we respect it (for example Gemma or Llama). The initial catalog is [TO BE VERIFIED].

How much can an operator earn?

We do not know and we give no figures. Pay is proportional to verified work (signed receipts), not a guaranteed income, and the alpha has no real-money settlement.

Is it really OpenAI-compatible?

The goal is the same API shape: /v1/chat/completions and /v1/models. The exact scope (streaming, tools, etc.) is in development and [TO BE VERIFIED] in the beta documentation.
